A sale usually goes better when the preparation begins before the sign goes up. These are the questions worth working through first.

## 1. What does this sale need to accomplish?

Start with the move, not the market. When do you need to be in your next home? Is there a purchase tied to this sale? Are there family members, advisors, or shared decisions involved? The answers shape nearly every other choice, from timing to preparation to which offer is the right one.

## 2. How will a buyer see the home?

Owners tend to stop noticing what buyers notice first: a worn entry, dated lighting, deferred maintenance, clutter in a favorite room. A candid walk-through, ideally with someone who sees many homes, helps you view the property with fresh eyes.

Consider the setting too. In West Michigan, a home's relationship to its surroundings, whether that is a lake, a river, wooded lot, or village center, is often part of the appeal and deserves to be shown well.

## 3. What preparation is actually worth doing?

Not every project pays off before a sale. A useful way to sort the list:

- **Safety and function.** Items that affect how the house works or how safe it is should usually be addressed.
- **First impressions.** Entry, light, cleanliness, and simplicity tend to carry outsized weight.
- **Cosmetic upgrades.** These are more discretionary. Spend only where a buyer would clearly notice and value the change.

## 4. How should the home be positioned?

Pricing, photography, presentation, and launch timing work best as one plan rather than separate decisions. The story of the property, what makes it distinctive and who it will appeal to, should guide all of them.

## 5. What happens around the sale?

A sale involves inspections, an appraisal, title work, and often repair conversations. Knowing what to expect in advance reduces surprises. It also helps to think ahead about the logistics of the transition, such as where you will go and how the move itself will work.

## 6. Who do you want helping you?

Look for someone who listens first, communicates without being chased, and pays attention to details. Ask how they will keep you informed and what they would do in your specific situation.
